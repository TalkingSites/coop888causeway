<div class="story">
<p class="lead">In 2016 Antony McMullen put that question in Pro Bono. Not long after, over a conversation at the Kelvin Club, his old friend and community development teacher Basil Varghese mentioned he had some office space going.</p>
<p>The dots connected. The idea became a space where smaller, socially minded enterprises and freelancers could come together, aiming in the spirit of Melbourne's eight-hours movement for a life-giving balance between labour, recreation and rest.</p>
<p>888 Co-operative Causeway formed in 2018, with Basil as its first patron. Today it's an affordable, volunteer-run co-working network in the Causeway Building, and a home for events and a podcast on co-operative and social economy ideas.</p>
</div>
<aside class="purpose">
<h2>Our purpose</h2>
<p>To support businesses and organisations in Melbourne that embody a co-operative spirit.</p>
<ul>
<li>Providing enabling infrastructure</li>
<li>Fostering a strong sense of community</li>
<li>Creating valuable networks for members</li>
</ul>
</aside>

<h2>Run by the people in it.</h2>
<p>This isn't your average co-working space. Members can vote at general meetings, stand for the board and have a say in the co-op's strategy and development. We're guided by the <a href="https://www.ica.coop/en/cooperatives/cooperative-identity" rel="noopener">International Co-operative Alliance's values and principles</a>.</p>
